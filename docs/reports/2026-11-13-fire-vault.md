# Purposeful fire acrobatics and battle movement

Every fourth fire attack can be a short handstand with alternating foot-fired Fireballs. Both use the existing projectile/collision/network path and each pays the normal 7 mana. Without the reserve for both shots the caster chooses a normal attack. The body plants its hands, inverts, releases at 0.64/0.88 seconds, and returns to its feet. Only this active pose probes its existing hand, foot and head anchors to maintain ground support; no additional meshes, materials or shaders are introduced.

Between strikes and during mana recovery, elemental mages change position through the ordinary collision/crowd steering. Water casters reposition while gathering; fire casters keep their cast gestures rooted. Blocked sidesteps try the other direction. A fire caster without sight approaches for a clear line instead of repeatedly performing attacks behind cover. Stun, carrying and levitation cancel a handstand before another foot shot.

The fireVault network mode is in the shared model mode list. Its hit capsule follows the current pelvis transform during inversion, using two endpoints from the same freshly updated pivot. Other NPC capsules are unchanged, and moved/hidden horse capsule regressions still pass. Returning to another mode clears the pelvis roll, including interrupted/dead states.

Validation: hand/head/foot ordering and support, real paid alternating foot origins, no duplicate or unaffordable releases, interruption, rotated capsule intersection after a move without rendering, existing elemental/earth/NPC regressions. The actual Creative replay used the real Game.frame, tracked hand and held wand on an open terrain clearing. The mage moved from his starting point, fired twice from raised feet (mana 173 → 166 → 159), and Jolt selected creative-fire:1 and completed with result hit during the inverted pose. The phase returned to Chamber after interruption. The screenshot was inspected; no page errors. Accelerated gameplay used matrix updates instead of intermediate GPU rendering, with the actual renderer for the image; this is not a GPU benchmark or multiplayer soak.
